## Overview

Between 94% and 98% of police responses to burglar alarms are false. A normal alarm only knows one thing — something moved — so it escalates on the only signal it has. The obvious fix, a camera that is always recording inside your house, is exactly what most people refuse to install.

Radar is a home that watches only when it has a reason to, and can prove to 911 that it had one. Built at VTHacks 14, it won **1st place for Best UI/UX** and **2nd place for GoDaddy's Best Use of Agent Name Service (ANS)**.

## How it works

- A laptop on the home Wi-Fi reads Channel State Information and notices motion.
- A servo rotates ninety degrees and pulls a physical shield off the camera lens — until then, the camera literally cannot see.
- A vision agent starts describing the room, and the resident gets that description on their Apple Watch in about three seconds.
- If the resident decides it is an emergency, they start the incident from their wrist. Only then does a caller agent dial 911 and talk to the dispatcher in plain English, driven by what the camera is seeing.
- When the call ends, the incident is sealed into a hash-chained record and sent to the responding department.

Radar never calls 911 on its own. The only thing that happens without a human is a shutter opening.

## What I built

- **Wi-Fi sensing module** that detects intruder movement from Channel State Information, the trigger for the whole system.
- **Master Agent** — the trust boundary and coordinator of a seven-agent backend. It classifies events, issues signed shutter grants, and coordinates the automated 911 call with live incident data.
- **iPhone and Apple Watch UI** in SwiftUI for real-time safety alerts.
- **Deployment** of the multi-agent backend and web dashboard on Vultr for real-time monitoring and post-incident review.

## Takeaway

Every agent has its own cryptographic identity, and identity decides whether a physical object moves. Designing a system where AI does the watching but a human makes the call taught me a lot about putting hard boundaries around autonomous agents.
